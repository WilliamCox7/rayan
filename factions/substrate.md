# Substrate

## Overview
- **Type:** Tech startup → Media/streaming conglomerate
- **Founded:** TBD
- **Headquarters:** San Francisco
- **Size:** Started small; now a major platform with political influence

## The Company

### Before the Pivot
Substrate was a game engine company competing at the bottom of the market—up against Unity, Unreal,
Godot, and losing. They had ambition, a decent tech stack, and not enough traction to survive long-term.

[Elia](../characters/elia.md) worked there as a software engineer, using their infrastructure after hours
to build [Rayan](../science/rayan.md)—a private simulation to watch his dead daughter grow up.

### The Discovery
Substrate discovered what Elia had built on their hardware. Legally, they owned it—anything created on
company equipment belongs to the company.

But instead of shutting it down or firing him, they realized what they had: a living, breathing world
with a genuinely autonomous character at its center. Something no game engine could produce. Something
people would pay to watch.

### The Pivot
Substrate pivoted from game engine to streaming platform, launching a service built around
[Rayan](../science/rayan.md).

## The Streaming Service

### Two Ways to Watch

**1. Produced Episodes**
- Edited, polished, narrative-driven
- Story arcs crafted by producers and editors
- Characters framed as heroes, villains, lovers, rivals
- Context added or removed to serve the narrative
- Aired on a schedule, like traditional TV
- The majority of viewers watch this way

**2. Live Streams**
- Raw, unedited feeds from any known character
- Viewers can watch in real-time, for hours
- No framing, no cuts—just life as it happens
- A smaller, more dedicated audience
- These viewers see the truth

### The Divide
Episode watchers accept the edit as reality. Live streamers know the difference. When the two groups
clash over "who a character really is," they're fighting over manufactured vs. actual truth.

See: [The Edit](../themes/the-edit.md)

## Political Influence

### Big Brother Element
Substrate sees everything. Every character, every moment, every conversation in Rayan. They choose what
to show and what to hide. They also track their audience—who watches what, who they root for, what makes
them engage.

The watchers think they're observing. They're also being observed.

### Narrative as Politics
The produced episodes don't just entertain. They shape how audiences think about:
- Power and authority (who deserves to rule?)
- Class and wealth (who deserves sympathy?)
- Conflict (how should disputes be resolved?)
- Morality (who are the good guys?)

Substrate has interests—commercial and political. The edit serves those interests. Not through explicit
propaganda, but through story. Through who gets to be the hero.

### The Meta Layer
This mirrors reality. The manipulation audiences experience watching Substrate's content reflects the
manipulation all media consumers experience: news edited for narrative, algorithms choosing what you see,
history written by whoever controls the story.

## Elia's Position

### Initially
Elia stayed at Substrate after the discovery. They needed his domain knowledge to keep Rayan running,
and he wouldn't leave Raya. He was trapped, complicit, but at least he was close to her.

### Eventually
Substrate fired him. Once they had extracted enough understanding of his systems, Elia became
disposable—or perhaps a liability. The creator was cut out of his creation.

Now [Elia](../characters/elia.md) watches Raya on the streaming service every day, like any other viewer.
He has no special access. No control. No way to reach her. He went from god to employee to audience
member.

## Raya's Status
Raya doesn't know. She continues living her life in Rayan, moving toward completing her unfinished task,
unaware that millions might be watching. She is, within the simulation, effectively immortal—she cannot
die, because the show must go on.
