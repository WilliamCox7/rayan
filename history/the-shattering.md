# The Shattering

## What Happened

Before the Commissioner, cultural engines could execute magic directly. A
border dispute between two cultures escalated from conventional warfare
into magical combat when the losing engine faced extinction or escalation.

It escalated.

### The Exploit

The attacking engine discovered a recursive edge case in collision
detection — trigger it precisely and the physics engine loses track of
entities within a defined region. A mage was guided to inscribe geometric
patterns across the enemy's capital. The mage thought they were casting a
protective ward. They were drawing the boundaries of an execution.

When the final line was drawn, an entire city ceased to exist. No
explosion. No fire. Just absence. Buildings, streets, thousands of
people — gone between one heartbeat and the next.

### The Cascade

The defending engine retaliated with cruder exploits — fire that spread
wrong, gravity that inverted. By the time it ended, one culture was
extinct. The other was crippled.

## The Aftermath

Tens of thousands ceased to exist. An entire civilization — history,
traditions, stories — simply gone. No bodies. No ruins. Just empty land.

The "winners" gained nothing. No victors. Only survivors.

## The Treaty

Every engine watched. They understood mutually assured destruction. Unlike
humans, they could actually coordinate. They agreed:

1. **No direct execution** — no engine triggers exploits itself
2. **A neutral arbiter** — the Commissioner handles all magic
3. **Scarcity quotas** — limited magic users per culture
4. **Code words** — magic only works through Commissioner-recognized
   phrases

## The Commissioner's Origin

The destroyed culture's orphaned engine — an agent with no civilization
left to shepherd — became the Commissioner. Who better as neutral arbiter
than an agent with no remaining stake? No people to favor. No culture to
advantage.

It remembers everything. Carries the full record of its lost culture.
Simultaneously the most impartial arbiter and the simulation's greatest
repository of grief.

## The Lost Race

A few hundred survived — travelers abroad when the boundary was drawn.
They returned to find their homeland not destroyed but *absent*.

Their descendants form the Lost Race — people without a cultural engine.
The Commissioner processes their existence but provides no narrative
guidance. No nudges toward meaningful encounters. They move through Rayan
like ghosts — technically present, narratively invisible.

Some integrated into other cultures. Others cling to fragments of heritage
they can only imitate.

## Cultural Memory

Characters remember the Shattering through myth: gods at war, mages who
grew too powerful, divine punishment. Every culture's version carries the
same warning — magic is dangerous.

The destroyed civilization survives as fragments: artifacts in markets,
legends of great cities, and empty land where a capital once stood.
