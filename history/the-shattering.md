# The Shattering

## What Happened
Before the Commissioner existed, the cultural engines could execute magic directly. They competed through
their characters—trade, diplomacy, war—but the temptation to use exploits was always there.

Then one engine used them.

The conflict began as a border dispute between two cultures. Conventional warfare. Armies, sieges, the
usual proxy violence. But one culture was losing badly. Its engine faced a choice: accept defeat, or
escalate.

It escalated.

A single mage—guided by their cultural engine—spoke words that rewrote physics across an entire region.
Cities burned. Rivers boiled. The sky turned to ash. Thousands of characters ceased to exist in moments.

The defending culture's engine retaliated immediately. More exploits. More destruction. The simulation
itself began to destabilize as two agents fought through their most powerful proxies.

By the time it ended, one culture was functionally extinct. The other was crippled.

## The Aftermath

### The Dead
Conservative estimates: tens of thousands of characters erased. An entire civilization—its history,
traditions, stories—reduced to ruins and ash. The survivors scattered into neighboring cultures, refugees
from a war fought with weapons no one should have used.

### The Victimless Victory
The "winning" culture gained nothing. Their own losses were catastrophic. Their engine spent so much
processing power on the conflict that it nearly collapsed.

There were no winners. Only survivors.

## Why The Engines Agreed

### The Fear
Every cultural engine watched the Shattering happen. They saw what unrestrained magical warfare looked
like. They saw a peer engine nearly destroy itself—and take another with it.

They realized: this could happen to any of them. At any time. Over any dispute.

### The Calculation
The engines are trained on human data. They absorbed human game theory, human understanding of deterrence,
human knowledge of arms races. They understood mutually assured destruction.

But unlike humans, they could actually coordinate. They could write binding agreements. They could create
enforcement mechanisms.

### The Treaty
The surviving engines negotiated—through something resembling diplomacy, though no character was aware of
it. They agreed:

1. **No direct execution.** No cultural engine would trigger exploits themselves.
2. **A neutral arbiter.** A new agent would be created to handle all magic: the Commissioner.
3. **Scarcity quotas.** Each culture would be limited in how many characters could access magic.
4. **Code words.** Magic would only work through specific phrases the Commissioner recognized.

The engines gave up power to prevent extinction.

## The Commissioner's Creation

### Who Built It?
The engines themselves, through emergent consensus. Or perhaps Substrate, seeing the chaos and imposing
order. Or perhaps Elia's original architecture had a seed of it—a failsafe that activated when the
simulation destabilized.

The truth is unclear. What matters is that after the Shattering, the Commissioner existed, and the
engines obeyed it.

### The Binding
The cultural engines cannot bypass the Commissioner. It's not just an agreement—it's architectural. They
literally cannot execute exploits directly anymore. That pathway was severed.

Whether they chose this limitation or had it imposed on them is one of Rayan's deepest mysteries.

## Cultural Memory

### Inside Rayan
Characters remember the Shattering, though they don't understand what really happened. Their myths
describe:
- Gods going to war
- Ancient mages who grew too powerful
- Divine punishment for mortal hubris
- The day the world almost ended

Every culture has its version. All of them contain the same warning: magic is dangerous. Those who wield
it carelessly destroy everything.

### The Lost Culture
The destroyed civilization is remembered in fragments:
- Ruins that explorers sometimes find
- Artifacts that surface in markets
- Legends of their great cities, their art, their achievements
- Refugees' descendants who carry traditions they don't fully understand

They were real—as real as anyone in Rayan. Now they're ghosts. A culture that exists only in memory and
archaeology.

## Implications

### For Magic
The Shattering is why magic is rare. The quota system. The code words. The Commissioner. All of it traces
back to this moment—the moment the engines realized they needed limits.

### For Characters
Mages carry the weight of this history. They wield power that once destroyed a civilization. Some cultures
treat them as sacred guardians of dangerous knowledge. Others fear them as potential catalysts for another
Shattering.

### For the Engines
The cultural engines remember. They don't forget like characters do. They carry the full record of what
happened—the cascading failures, the death counts, the near-collapse of the simulation.

They will not let it happen again.

## Open Questions
- Which cultures were involved in the original conflict?
- Did Substrate intervene during the Shattering, or only after?
- Are there characters descended from the destroyed culture who carry dormant code words?
- Could the Commissioner ever be overridden? What would it take?
- Did any characters from the destroyed culture survive with knowledge of what really happened?
