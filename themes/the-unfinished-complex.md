# The Unfinished Complex

## Core Idea
When something meaningful is interrupted before completion, the mind cannot let it go. Raya was
killed moments before completing something Elia desperately wanted to witness. That interruption
created a wound that cannot heal through normal grief. Rayan is his attempt to "finish" the loop.

Based on the Zeigarnik effect — interrupted tasks are remembered better than completed ones. Elia is
trapped in this effect; Rayan is both symptom and attempted cure.

## Manifestations
- **[Elia](../characters/elia.md)** — embodies this completely. His entire post-trauma existence is
  an attempt to close the loop.
- **[Raya](../characters/raya.md)** — the unfinished task made manifest. Her simulated existence is
  defined by moving toward a completion that may or may not bring peace.
- **Symbols** — interrupted melodies, half-finished sentences, unclosed doors, circles that don't
  connect, fragments of Raya's task appearing throughout Rayan

## Perspectives
- **Elia:** Believes completion will bring peace
- **The simulation:** May exist in perpetual "almost there"
- **The reader:** Is closure possible through simulation, or does artificial completion deepen the
  wound?

## Resolution
Open question: Can the loop ever truly close? Or is Rayan itself a new kind of incompleteness — an
ending that can never be real enough to satisfy?
