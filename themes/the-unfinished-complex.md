# The Unfinished Complex

## Core Idea
When something meaningful is interrupted before completion, the mind cannot let it go. The unfinished task
loops endlessly, demanding resolution. This is the psychological engine that drives the entire story.

Raya was killed moments before completing something Elia desperately wanted to witness. That interruption
created a wound that cannot heal through normal grief. The simulation—Rayan—is his attempt to "finish"
what was left incomplete—to finally witness the ending he was denied.

## Manifestations

### Characters
- [Elia](../characters/elia.md) — He embodies this theme completely. His entire post-trauma existence is
  an attempt to close the loop.
- [Raya](../characters/raya.md) — She is the unfinished task made manifest. Her simulated existence is
  defined by moving toward a completion that may or may not bring peace.

### Plot Points
<!-- Key scenes or events that explore this theme -->
- The moment of Raya's death / the interruption
- Elia's decision to build Rayan
- The simulated Raya approaching her goal
- The moment of completion (if it comes)—does it bring closure or emptiness?

### Symbols & Motifs
<!-- Recurring images, objects, or patterns that reinforce it -->
- Interrupted melodies, half-finished sentences, unclosed doors
- Circles that don't connect
- The thing Raya was about to do appearing in fragments throughout Rayan

## Perspectives
- **Elia:** Believes completion will bring peace
- **The simulation:** May exist in a state of perpetual "almost there"
- **The reader:** Is closure possible through simulation? Or does the artificial completion only deepen
  the wound?

## Resolution
<!-- How is this theme resolved or left open? What's the takeaway? -->
Open question: Can the loop ever truly close? Or is Rayan itself a new kind of incompleteness—an ending
that can never be real enough to satisfy?

## Notes
This connects to the Zeigarnik effect—the psychological phenomenon where interrupted tasks are remembered
better than completed ones. Elia is trapped in this effect, and Rayan is both a symptom and an attempted
cure.
