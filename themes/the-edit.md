# The Edit

## Core Idea
Not everyone sees the same Rayan. The world is real—but the truth is curated.

[Substrate](../factions/substrate.md) offers two ways to experience Rayan:

1. **Produced Episodes** — Edited, polished, narrative-driven. Story arcs crafted by producers. Characters
   framed as heroes or villains. Context removed or added. Aired on a schedule.

2. **Live Streams** — Raw, unedited feeds. Viewers can watch any character they know about, in real-time.
   No framing. No cuts. Just life as it happens.

The episode watchers accept the edit as truth. The live streamers see what's really happening—and they
know the difference.

## The Divide

### Episode Watchers
- Consume Rayan as entertainment
- Trust the narrative Substrate presents
- Have "favorite characters" based on how they're portrayed
- React to manufactured drama, unaware it's manufactured
- The majority of the audience

### Live Streamers
- A smaller, more dedicated community
- Watch the raw feeds, often for hours
- Notice when episodes contradict reality
- Develop parasocial relationships with characters based on actual behavior
- Sometimes clash with episode watchers over "who a character really is"

## The Manipulation

### How Substrate Edits
- **Selective framing:** A character's cruelty becomes "complexity." A kind act is cut for pacing.
- **Narrative arcs:** Real events restructured to fit story beats—rising action, climax, resolution
- **Villain creation:** Someone unpopular with focus groups gets unflattering edits
- **Romance manufacturing:** Two characters who barely interact get edited into a love story
- **Context removal:** A justified action looks monstrous without the preceding scene

### Why It Matters
The episode watchers aren't stupid. They're trusting a system that claims to show reality. They don't
know the edit exists—or they assume it's minor. "It's just for pacing." "They wouldn't lie."

But Substrate has interests. Political interests. Commercial interests. The edit serves those interests.

## Political Influence
Substrate doesn't just entertain. The narratives they promote shape how audiences think about:
- Power and authority
- Class and wealth
- Conflict resolution
- Who deserves sympathy

The world inside Rayan becomes a political tool. Not through explicit propaganda—through story. Through
who the heroes are. Through which conflicts get resolved and how.

## The Meta Layer
This mirrors reality. Audiences watching Substrate's edited content are experiencing what all media
consumers experience:
- News edited for narrative
- Social media algorithms choosing what you see
- History written by whoever controls the story

When live streamers argue with episode watchers about "the truth," they're reenacting debates happening
in the real world right now.

## Big Brother Element
Substrate sees everything. Every character, every moment, every conversation. They choose what to show
and what to hide. The audience is surveilled through their viewing habits. Substrate knows who watches
what, who they root for, what makes them engage.

The watchers think they're observing. They're also being observed.
