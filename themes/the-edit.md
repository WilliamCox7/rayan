# The Edit

## Core Idea
Not everyone sees the same Rayan. The world is real — but the truth is curated.

## Two Experiences
- **Produced episodes** — edited, polished, narrative-driven. Story arcs crafted by producers.
  Characters framed as heroes or villains. Context removed or added. The majority watches these.
- **Live streams** — raw, unedited feeds of any character in real-time. No framing. A smaller,
  dedicated community that sees what's really happening and knows the difference.

## How Substrate Edits
- Selective framing (cruelty becomes "complexity"; kind acts cut for pacing)
- Narrative arcs imposed on real events
- Villain creation through unflattering edits
- Romance manufacturing between characters who barely interact
- Context removal (justified actions look monstrous without the preceding scene)

## The Divide
Episode watchers trust the presented narrative, have favorites based on portrayal, react to
manufactured drama. Live streamers notice discrepancies, develop relationships with characters based
on actual behavior, clash with episode watchers over "who a character really is."

## Political Influence
Substrate shapes how audiences think about power, class, conflict, and morality — not through
propaganda but through story. Through who the heroes are. Through which conflicts get resolved and
how. They also track audience viewing habits — the watchers are being watched.

## Meta Layer
Mirrors real media: news edited for narrative, algorithms choosing what you see, history written by
whoever controls the story. When live streamers argue with episode watchers about "the truth," they
reenact debates happening in the real world right now.
