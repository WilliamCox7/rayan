# Simulation Theory

## Core Idea
What if reality isn't real? What if everything—the world, the people, the laws of nature—is a construct?
And if it is... does it matter?

In [Rayan](../science/rayan.md), this isn't philosophy. It's literal truth. The world is a simulation.
The inhabitants don't know. But some might sense something is off.

## How It Manifests

### The Patterns
The [world engine](../science/ai-architecture.md) runs all NPCs from a single AI substrate. This could
create subtle tells:
- Characters who are too thematically coherent
- Conversations that feel scripted in hindsight
- Coincidences that serve the narrative too perfectly
- Moments of déjà vu or uncanny repetition

### The Edges
What happens at the boundaries of the world?
- Places no one has been—do they exist before someone goes there?
- Memories that don't quite add up
- History that feels written rather than lived

### The Watchers
The streaming audience observes Rayan constantly. Could that observation affect reality?
- Characters who feel watched but can't explain why
- A sense that their lives have stakes beyond what they can see
- The unsettling feeling of being entertainment

## Characters and Suspicion
Some characters might be more prone to questioning:
- Philosophers, scholars, the neurotically observant
- Those who've experienced glitches or inconsistencies
- Anyone who gets too close to [Raya](../characters/raya.md) and notices she's... different

## The Question
If you discovered your world was a simulation:
- Would you try to break free?
- Would you despair?
- Would you decide it doesn't matter—that your experiences are real regardless of substrate?
- Would you try to find the creator?

## Connection to Elia
[Elia](../characters/elia.md) built this world to give his daughter a life. But he also populated it with
people who believe they're real. If any of them ever discovered the truth, what would they think of their
god—a grieving man in San Francisco, watching them on a screen?
