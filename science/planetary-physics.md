# Planetary Physics

## The Design

[Elia](../characters/elia.md) built [Rayan](rayan.md) after hours on a
work computer. He wasn't simulating novel physics — he was minimizing
complexity.

- **Planetary scale:** Pluto-sized (~2,377 km diameter vs Earth's ~12,742)
- **Physics engine:** Stock Earth (9.8 m/s² gravity, 24-hour day, standard
  atmosphere)
- **Result:** A small world that *feels* normal to inhabit

Small planet = less rendering. Stock physics = no edge cases. Engineering
pragmatism, not philosophy.

## What Feels Normal

Everything about physics: gravity, falling, weather, day/night, how bodies
move and tire. A character dropped here would notice nothing wrong.

## What Feels Wrong

The geometry betrays the small scale:

- **Close horizon** — ~2-3 km away (vs ~5 km on Earth). Curve visible
  from any elevation.
- **Finite world** — Circumnavigation in a lifetime. Every culture within
  reach of every other. Explorers run out of world.
- **Navigation oddities** — Ships vanish over the horizon fast.
  Constellations barely shift when traveling. Maps can depict the *entire*
  world accurately.

## Narrative Implications

Most characters never question it — it's all they've known. But perceptive
characters might notice, feeding into
[simulation theory](../themes/simulation-theory.md) themes.

The small scale keeps [cultural engines](ai-architecture.md) in constant
proximity. Border friction is permanent. May have contributed to the
pressure behind [the Shattering](../history/the-shattering.md).
